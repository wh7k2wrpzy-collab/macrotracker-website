Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 22, 2026

This policy explains what the MacroTracker: Food & Macros app for iPhone (“MacroTracker”, “the app”) does with your information.

In short

  • What you track is saved on your iPhone. If you choose to sign in, a copy is also kept in your own private iCloud storage.
  • We do not run servers that receive your tracking data, and we cannot see it.
  • The app has no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and includes no third-party software kits.
  • Your saved foods and the app’s built-in restaurant nutrition data are searched on your iPhone. When you tap Search, your search words are sent to two public food databases, USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts. Scanning a barcode sends the barcode number to Open Food Facts only.
  • Apple handles sign-in, iCloud storage, the free trial and purchases under its own terms.
  • You can delete your data from inside the app at any time.

1. Who we are

MacroTracker is published by James Hornsby, an individual developer. In this policy “we” and “us” mean that developer. You can reach us at support@macrotracker.health.

2. What the app stores on your iPhone

MacroTracker does not ask you to create an account with us. Everything you enter is saved in the app on your iPhone so that the app can show it back to you:

  • Foods and your food log: the foods you save (name, brand, barcode if it has one, serving and nutrition values) and what you log each day (food, amount, meal, date and time, calories and nutrients).
  • Water, weight and body measurements: each entry with its amount and date and time.
  • Goals: your calorie goal, macro split and water goal, including earlier goals, so past days are still measured against the goals you had then.
  • Preferences: units, appearance, which optional features are switched on, and whether you have seen the introduction and the free trial offer.
  • Trial and purchase status: a note of the date your free trial started, taken from Apple’s App Store record once you have started the trial; the most recent date the app has seen (used to keep the trial honest if the clock is changed); and whether the lifetime purchase is owned. Apple holds the trial and purchase records themselves (see section 8).
  • Sync housekeeping: if you have used iCloud sync, when it last ran and a random identifier that lets the app tell whether your data has since been deleted from another device.

Streaks and badges are worked out from your entries each time they are shown. They are not stored separately.

If you sign in, your Apple sign-in identifier and name are kept in the iPhone’s Keychain, a protected storage area provided by iOS. The app does not keep its trial note there. See Deleting your data for what that means when you remove the app.

3. What the app uses but does not keep

The goal calculator asks for your age, height, weight, sex, activity level and, optionally, body-fat percentage. These answers are used on your iPhone to work out an estimate and are then discarded. They are not saved and not sent anywhere. Only the calorie and macro goals you choose to save are kept.

4. Camera

The app asks for camera access only so that you can scan barcodes and nutrition labels. You can use the app without it.

  • Barcodes are read on your iPhone. No picture is kept. Only the barcode number is used, to look the product up (see section 7).
  • Nutrition labels are photographed and the text is read on your iPhone using Apple’s on-device text recognition. The photo is not saved to your photo library, not stored by the app and not sent anywhere.

The app does not request access to your location, contacts, photo library, microphone, Apple Health data, or notifications.

5. Optional iCloud sync

If you sign in (see section 6), MacroTracker copies your tracking data to your own private iCloud storage so it is backed up and kept in step across your devices. If you do not sign in, the app does not sync.

  • What syncs: your saved foods, food log, water, weight and measurement entries, the list of measurement types, and your goals. Entries you have removed are synced as “removed” so they disappear from your other devices too.
  • What does not sync through the app: your preferences. Your free trial and lifetime purchase are not part of iCloud sync either: they are App Store records that Apple keeps with your Apple Account (see section 8).
  • Where it goes: into the private database of an iCloud container that belongs to MacroTracker, in the iCloud account signed in on your iPhone. The app never writes your data to a public or shared database.
  • Who can see it: the data is stored by Apple in your iCloud account. We do not have access to it and cannot read, recover or delete it for you.
  • A deletion marker: if you use Delete My Data, the app writes a small marker to your private iCloud database. It contains a random identifier and a date, and nothing about what you tracked. Its purpose is to stop another of your devices from putting deleted data back.

Your synced data counts toward your own iCloud storage and is handled by Apple under Apple’s Privacy Policy and iCloud terms. MacroTracker relies on the protection Apple provides for iCloud data and does not add separate encryption of its own.

6. Sign in with Apple

Signing in is optional and is used only to turn on iCloud sync. You do not need to sign in to use or buy the app.

  • What the app receives from Apple: an identifier that Apple creates for you for this app and, the first time you sign in and if you choose to share it, your name. The app does not ask for your email address and never receives your Apple Account password.
  • Where it is kept: in the Keychain on your iPhone, so the app can remember that you are signed in and show your name.
  • What we receive: nothing. We do not operate an account server, and the identifier and name are not sent to us.

Apple knows that you use Sign in with Apple with MacroTracker and handles that information under its own privacy policy. Signing out stops syncing; it does not delete the data on your iPhone or the copy already in your iCloud.

7. Food search and barcode lookup

MacroTracker’s food search looks in four places. Two are on your iPhone and two are online:

  • Your saved foods are searched on your iPhone as you type. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • The built-in restaurant nutrition data is searched on your iPhone as you type (see below). Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts, two online food databases, are searched only when you tap Search on the keyboard, not as you type.

Apart from Apple’s services, these two food databases are the only third-party services the app contacts, and only when you submit an online search, scan a barcode, or open a product from their results.

USDA FoodData Central

USDA FoodData Central is a food and nutrient database run by the U.S. Department of Agriculture.

  • When it is contacted: when you submit an online food search, including a search by name after a barcode could not be found, and when you open one of its results to see the details.
  • What is sent: the words you search for, or the FoodData Central number of a result you open. Barcode scans are not sent to USDA; they are looked up in Open Food Facts only.
  • How it is sent: directly from your iPhone to USDA’s API service, over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. We do not run a server in between, so we do not receive these requests.
  • The app’s key: each request includes an API key that belongs to the app. It identifies MacroTracker, not you. You do not need a USDA account, and the app does not use one.
  • Technical information: USDA’s API service may receive your device’s public IP address and standard technical information associated with the network request.
  • What is not sent: your name, your Apple identifier, your food log, your weight or measurements, your goals, foods you created yourself, or anything read from a nutrition-label scan.

USDA handles what it receives under its own policies, which we do not control.

Open Food Facts

To find packaged foods and look up barcodes, MacroTracker also uses Open Food Facts, a public food database run by a non-profit organisation. It is contacted only when you submit an online search, scan a barcode, or open a product from its results.

  • What is sent: the words you search for, or the barcode number.
  • What is sent with every request: the name and version of the app and our support email address, which Open Food Facts asks apps to include so it can contact the developer. This is our address, not yours.
  • What Open Food Facts can also see: as with any internet request, the IP address of your device, and ordinary technical details of the connection.
  • Product photos shown in results are downloaded directly from Open Food Facts and may be cached on your iPhone for a while.
  • What is not sent: your name, your Apple identifier, your food log, your weight or measurements, your goals, foods you created yourself, or anything read from a nutrition-label scan.

Open Food Facts handles what it receives under its own terms and privacy policy, which we do not control. Its food data is made available under the Open Database License.

Built-in restaurant nutrition data

  • MacroTracker includes nutrition information for menu items from a number of restaurant chains. It is stored in the app on your iPhone.
  • Searching it does not need an internet connection. Your search words are not sent to us, to any restaurant or to a restaurant’s website.
  • A restaurant item you choose works like any other food: if you add it to your foods or log it, it is saved in the app with the rest of your data (see section 2), and synced if you have turned on iCloud sync.
  • Some items include a link to the restaurant’s own nutrition information. If you tap it, the restaurant’s website opens in your web browser, and that website’s own privacy practices apply.

8. Trial and purchases

  • Free trial: the 14-day free trial does not start by itself. You start it by tapping Start 14-Day Free Trial in the app and confirming through Apple’s App Store. The trial is a free App Store item, so Apple processes the transaction and keeps a record of it with your Apple Account, as it does for purchases. The app asks Apple’s purchase system on your iPhone whether that record exists and when it began, and saves a note of that date in the app’s settings on your iPhone. That note is not sent to us.
  • Other devices and reinstalling: because Apple’s record of the trial belongs to your Apple Account, the app can find it again on another device or after it is reinstalled. Deleting your data, reinstalling the app or restoring purchases does not start a new trial.
  • Lifetime purchase: the purchase is made through Apple’s App Store. Apple processes the payment; we never receive your card or payment details. The app asks Apple’s purchase system on your iPhone whether the purchase is owned, and remembers the answer on your iPhone.
  • What we receive from Apple: like all App Store developers, we receive sales reports from Apple about App Store transactions. They do not identify you to us.

10. What the app does not do

  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics or usage tracking in the app.
  • No third-party software development kits, and no third-party crash-reporting tools.
  • No selling of personal information. We do not receive your tracking data, so we have none to sell or share.

Apple may give developers aggregated statistics, such as download counts and crash reports, if you have chosen to share them in your iPhone’s settings. Those come from Apple, not from code in the app.

11. Deleting your data

Removing individual entries

You can remove any entry in the app. A removed entry is hidden straight away and marked as removed. So that the removal can reach your other devices, the removed record is kept in the background, on your iPhone and in your iCloud if you sync, until you use Delete My Data.

Delete My Data

In the app, go to Settings and choose Delete My Data. After you confirm, the app:

  1. turns off MacroTracker iCloud sync on that iPhone;
  2. deletes MacroTracker’s storage in your private iCloud, including records of earlier removals, and checks with iCloud that it is gone;
  3. only then permanently erases all tracking data on the iPhone, including entries that had only been marked as removed;
  4. signs you out and removes your Apple sign-in identifier and name from the Keychain;
  5. resets your preferences and returns the app to its introduction.

Things you should know about it:

  • If iCloud cannot be reached or the deletion cannot be confirmed, the app tells you so and deletes nothing on your iPhone. You can try again or keep your data.
  • If your iPhone has synced before but is no longer signed in to iCloud, the app cannot delete or check your iCloud copy. It will say so. You can then choose to delete the data on that iPhone only; in that case your iCloud copy, if there is one, is not deleted.
  • Other devices. Another device that still holds your data keeps its own copy until you use Delete My Data on it as well. The app is designed to stop that device from syncing its copy back to iCloud.
  • What is kept: Apple’s records of your free trial and of your lifetime purchase, which remain with your Apple Account, and the app’s own note of them on that iPhone. The lifetime purchase can be restored. The free trial is not restarted.
  • Sign in with Apple. The app cannot withdraw your Apple sign-in on Apple’s side. To do that, open the Settings app on your iPhone, tap your name, then Sign in with Apple, choose MacroTracker and stop using it. The exact wording varies between iOS versions.

Deleting the app

Deleting the app from your iPhone removes the data stored in it on that iPhone. It does not delete your iCloud copy. If you were signed in, your Apple sign-in identifier and name, which are kept in the Keychain, may remain on the device after the app is deleted. Apple’s records of your free trial and of a lifetime purchase stay with your Apple Account. To remove everything the app can remove, use Delete My Data before deleting the app.

Copies we cannot delete

  • Device backups. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, backups made before a deletion may still contain your MacroTracker data. Restoring such a backup can bring that data back onto the device.
  • Removing the data in iCloud settings. If you delete MacroTracker’s data from your iPhone’s iCloud storage settings instead of using the app, the app will pause syncing and ask you what to do. It will not put your data back into iCloud by itself.
  • App Store records. Apple’s records of your free trial and of a lifetime purchase are held by Apple with your Apple Account. Delete My Data does not remove them, and we cannot delete them.
  • USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts. Searches and lookups already sent to USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts, including barcode lookups sent to Open Food Facts, cannot be recalled by the app. Delete My Data does not remove any records those services keep.

12. How long data is kept

Your data stays on your iPhone, and in your iCloud if you sync, until you delete it. We do not hold a copy, so we have no retention period of our own for your tracking data. Apple keeps its App Store records of your trial and purchase under its own policies. Emails you send us are kept for as long as needed to deal with your request and for our records.

13. Security

Your data is protected by the security of your iPhone, including its passcode and the protections iOS applies to app data and the Keychain, and by Apple’s security for iCloud. Connections the app makes to iCloud, to USDA FoodData Central and to Open Food Facts use encrypted connections. No method of storing or sending information is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. Keeping your iPhone and Apple Account secure is the most effective protection for your data.

14. Children

MacroTracker is a general nutrition tracking app. It is not designed for or directed at children. Its goal calculator is designed for adults and only accepts ages of 18 and over. The app does not otherwise ask for or record your age, and it has no accounts with us. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information by email, contact us and we will delete it.

15. Where data is processed

Your tracking data is stored on your iPhone and, if you sync, on Apple’s iCloud servers, wherever Apple operates them. Requests to USDA FoodData Central are handled by USDA’s API service, which is run for the U.S. government. Requests to Open Food Facts are handled on that organisation’s servers. These may be in a different country from the one you live in.

16. Your choices and requests

  • You can use the app without signing in, without syncing and without granting camera access.
  • You can see, change and delete your data in the app at any time.
  • Depending on where you live, you may have legal rights to access, correct or delete personal information held about you. Because we do not hold your tracking data, we cannot retrieve or delete it for you; the controls in the app and in your Apple Account are the way to do that. If you have emailed us and want that correspondence deleted, contact us.

17. This website and email

This website has no accounts, forms, cookies or analytics of our own. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, may log technical information such as IP addresses when pages are requested, under its own privacy statement. If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and use them only to reply and to keep a record of the request.

If this policy changes, we will post the new version on this page with a new effective date. If a change is significant, we will take reasonable steps to make it noticeable, for example in the app or its App Store listing.
