5. Accuracy of food information

  • Open Food Facts results, including every barcode lookup, come from a public database that anyone can contribute to. Entries may be incomplete, out of date or wrong.
  • USDA FoodData Central results come from the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s food database. Entries may be incomplete, and values for brand-name and restaurant foods may have changed since they were recorded.
  • Built-in restaurant nutrition data was collected from nutrition information that restaurants published. Restaurants change recipes, portions, serving sizes and menu items over time. Some records are older and may not reflect current menus, some do not list every value, and values may have been recorded incorrectly. The app shows a warning on some items whose information may be out of date, but not every out-of-date item is marked.
  • Different sources can list different values for the same or similar foods.
  • Nutrition-label scans are read automatically from a photo and can be misread. The app shows what it read so you can check and correct it before saving.
  • Foods you enter yourself are only as accurate as what you type.

We do not promise that any food information in the app is current, complete or error-free. Always check nutrition values against the product’s own packaging, or the restaurant’s current nutrition information, particularly when accuracy is important to you. MacroTracker does not provide allergen or ingredient-safety information and must not be relied on for allergies or dietary restrictions.
